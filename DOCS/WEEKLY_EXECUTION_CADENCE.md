# Weekly Execution Cadence

Last updated: 2026-03-19
Owner: FTC HOLDING / Una Labs

## Purpose

This cadence exists to turn the dashboard, triage system, and dual-lane tracker into a repeatable rhythm.

The goal is not perfect productivity.
The goal is consistent execution with less drift.

## Core Principle

Every week should have:
1. one money priority
2. one product priority
3. one leverage priority
4. a clear follow-up rhythm
5. a reset point before new chaos accumulates

If everything is urgent, nothing is prioritized.

---

## Weekly Structure

### Monday - Set Direction
Use Monday to decide what the week is actually about.

#### Actions
- review the Founder Dashboard
- review the Dual-Lane Tracker
- identify one money move
- identify one product move
- identify one leverage move
- list top follow-ups due
- remove stale tasks

#### Output
A week with only a few real priorities.

---

### Tuesday to Thursday - Execute and Advance
These are the primary movement days.

#### Actions
- do outreach
- follow up on open conversations
- move one product milestone forward
- complete one leverage improvement if still active
- update statuses after real actions

#### Rules
- do not re-decide the whole week every day
- do not let interesting new ideas replace active commitments
- use the triage system instead of activating new work impulsively

---

### Friday - Review and Reset
Friday is for cleanup, signal review, and deciding what carries forward.

#### Actions
- review sales lane results
- review job lane results
- review product move progress
- review leverage move progress
- document what stalled and why
- decide what carries into next week
- park what no longer deserves attention

#### Output
A cleaner next week, not a pile of unresolved tabs.

---

## Daily Rhythm

### Morning
- open the Founder Dashboard
- choose today’s money move
- choose today’s job move
- confirm the current product move
- confirm current follow-ups

### Midday
- check whether real movement happened
- if not, return to the most concrete next step
- do not spend midday inventing a new system to avoid work

### End of day
- update statuses
- set next actions
- log follow-ups due
- remove fake tasks

---

## Weekly Review Questions

### Money lane
- Did outreach happen consistently?
- Which niche responded best?
- Which message or offer landed best?
- What is the clearest path to one paid client next week?

### Job lane
- Were applications aligned or scattered?
- Did outreach happen to real fit roles or generic listings?
- Is the LinkedIn / positioning narrative improving?
- Where did conversations stall?

### Product lane
- Did one real product move happen?
- Was product work aligned with current strategy?
- Did product work support positioning, validation, or roadmap?

### Leverage lane
- Did one change reduce repeat confusion or manual effort?
- Was the leverage work worth the time spent?
- What should be systemized next versus left alone?

---

## Reset Rules

Use these when things begin to sprawl again.

### Reset if:
- more than 3 real priorities are active
- follow-ups are not being tracked
- new ideas keep interrupting current work
- dashboard is bloated
- client lane and job lane are mentally blending together

### Reset by:
- rewriting the dashboard down to current truth
- parking non-essential work
- re-running idea triage
- choosing one money move first

---

## When To Shift Priorities

### Shift toward revenue if:
- cash pressure increases
- active outreach is producing signal
- a prospect is close to paying

### Shift toward job lane if:
- strong-fit roles are open now
- recruiter or interview momentum appears
- stability opportunity is real and timely

### Shift toward product lane if:
- a flagship milestone supports distribution, trust, or strategy
- product movement is needed for credibility or roadmap integrity

### Shift toward leverage if:
- repeated confusion is slowing everything else
- one small system would reduce many future decisions

---

## Anti-Drift Rules

- Do not start a new week without choosing a money move.
- Do not let product work expand just because it feels meaningful.
- Do not use leverage work as sophisticated procrastination.
- Do not treat being busy as evidence of progress.
- Do not carry vague tasks forward week after week.
