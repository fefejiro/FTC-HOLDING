# Job Search Resume Lanes

Last updated: 2026-03-19
Owner: Mike / FTC HOLDING

## Purpose

This document defines the core resume lanes for Mike's current job search.

Goal:
- sound human
- stay aligned with real experience
- avoid AI-sounding fluff
- make tailoring faster
- support upward movement toward IT Director / technical leadership

## Core principle

Do not create a totally different personality for every role.
Create one true professional story with multiple role-shaped versions.

These lanes should be the base for targeted resumes, LinkedIn edits, and cover language.

---

## Lane 1 - IT Director / Business Systems Leadership

### Best-fit role types
- IT Director
- Director of Business Systems
- Technology Operations Lead
- Director of IT Strategy
- Head of Business Technology

### Positioning summary
Mike is a practical technology leader who improves systems, reduces operational friction, and drives execution across infrastructure, automation, product delivery, and internal workflows. He is strongest where technical depth and operational leadership need to meet.

### Human-language profile
I lead technology work with an operator mindset. My background spans IT management, systems architecture, product execution, and workflow improvement, with a strong bias toward practical delivery over theory. I work well in environments where technology needs to support real business movement, not just maintenance.

### Core strengths to emphasize
- IT leadership and systems ownership
- business systems and workflow improvement
- cross-functional execution
- vendor/platform coordination
- operational stability and decision-making
- automation and process simplification

### What to downplay
- experimental founder language that sounds scattered
- too much emphasis on side projects without business framing
- abstract AI enthusiasm without operational context

### Strong resume tone
- calm
- credible
- operational
- leadership-forward
- systems-minded

## Lane 2 - Solutions Architect / Automation Lead

### Best-fit role types
- Solutions Architect
- AI Automation Lead
- Enterprise Architect
- Workflow Automation Lead
- Business Systems Architect
- Technical Solutions Lead

### Positioning summary
Mike designs and delivers practical systems that connect business needs to real implementation. He is strongest in roles that require technical architecture, workflow design, automation thinking, and clear execution across messy environments.

### Human-language profile
I build and improve systems that help teams move faster and work with less friction. My work spans automation, architecture, product implementation, and operational problem-solving. I tend to do best where someone needs both technical understanding and the ability to turn ideas into working systems quickly.

### Core strengths to emphasize
- systems architecture
- workflow design and automation
- translating business needs into implementation
- fast MVP / practical build execution
- technical problem-solving
- internal tool and process design

### What to downplay
- overly executive language if the role is hands-on
- founder branding that distracts from implementation credibility
- product vision language without architecture substance

### Strong resume tone
- practical
- technical
- grounded
- implementation-focused
- solution-oriented

## Shared truth across all lanes

These are the real through-lines that should remain consistent:
- builder + operator
- practical execution over theory
- systems-minded
- strong with automation and process improvement
- credible across IT, product, and implementation
- able to move messy work toward clarity and delivery

## Shared language rules

### Use language like this
- built
- improved
- led
- implemented
- streamlined
- launched
- reduced
- delivered
- coordinated
- owned

### Avoid language like this
- leveraged cutting-edge AI synergies
- transformed ecosystems through innovation
- visionary thought leader
- revolutionary disruption
- hyper-scaled
- world-class ninja/rockstar language

## Human-tone rule

Every resume should sound like a real person describing real work.
If a sentence sounds like it was generated to impress recruiters instead of communicate truth, rewrite it.

---

## Tailoring rule

When a real job appears:
1. choose the best lane first
2. keep 80-90% of the lane intact
3. tailor the summary, skills emphasis, and key bullets
4. only add keywords that genuinely fit the experience

Do not rewrite from scratch unless the role is materially different.
