# PeacePad Journal Editorial Guide

## Purpose

The PeacePad Journal provides original, practical, child-first guidance for everyday co-parenting coordination. It does not provide legal, medical, mental-health, emergency, or custody advice.

## Voice and boundaries

- Use calm, gender-neutral language; describe actions, needs, and choices without assigning blame.
- Do not imply that a tool can make a person safe, prove a legal case, determine custody, or resolve abuse.
- Keep children out of adult conflict: never recommend that a child pass messages, report on a parent, or choose sides.
- Write in short, usable steps. Avoid clinical, legal, or diagnostic claims unless they are attributable to a qualified source and reviewed.

## Editorial workflow

1. Draft content in the structured article model with a category, summary, source list, and status.
2. Publish only general, original content that has passed editorial review.
3. Mark content about abuse, coercive control, safety planning, legal process, high conflict, or child protection as `review` until a qualified reviewer confirms it and current Canadian resources are linked.
4. Re-check external support links before every production release and at least quarterly.
5. Correct or unpublish misleading, outdated, or unsafe material promptly.

## Sensitive-topic standard

Safety content must say what it cannot do. It must not encourage unsafe evidence collection, confrontation, account sharing, tracking, or browser-history assumptions. Canadian public resources currently used by PeacePad include Canada.ca family-violence services and ShelterSafe safety-planning guidance.

## Measurement and privacy

Do not place user-content tracking, session replay, or sensitive-topic analytics in journal pages. If aggregate measurement is later approved, exclude Safety and Browse Safely routes and document the consent and retention model before release.
